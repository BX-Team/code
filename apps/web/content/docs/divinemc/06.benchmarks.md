DivineMC 26.2 was measured against Paper and Purpur with [mc-bench](https://github.com/NONPLAYT/mc-bench), an open harness that boots every server on the same world, drives it with the same bots and records every tick. Everything on this page can be reproduced from the published builds and configs.

## Summary

- **With 150 players and 1,500 mobs**, DivineMC with regionized chunk ticking ticks in 15.4 ms against Paper's 37.5 ms, 59% lower. The `max` profile is close behind at 17.2 ms.
- **Across eight busy, separated regions**, regionized chunk ticking brings the tick from 21.7 ms to 8.4 ms, 61% lower, with four worker threads.
- **In chunk pregeneration**, the `max` profile is 7% faster than Paper and spends 15% less CPU doing it.
- **The speed comes from using more cores.** DivineMC spreads the tick over worker threads, so it burns more CPU than Paper in the tick scenarios: +16% on `max`, +31% with RCT. With a thread pool idling next to the main thread, that is usually the right trade. On a small shared VPS it may not be.

| | |

| Server | pinned to the V-Cache CCD: 8 cores / 16 threads |
| Bot client | [SoulFire](https://github.com/soulfiremc-com/SoulFire), 12 GB heap, pinned to the other CCD |
| Minecraft | 26.2 |
| Server heap | 8 GB, G1 |
| Simulation / view distance | 10 / 10 |
| Date | 2026-09-28 |

| Server | Build |
|---|---|
| Paper | [26.2 #129](https://fill-data.papermc.io/v1/objects/b1d8f6bfa1b6101fa8e947b53041cb3bdf5540e7b83b6547ca19ba7edefeb083/paper-26.2-129.jar) |

| DivineMC | [26.2 #15](https://files.bxteam.org/divinemc/versions/26.2/15/divinemc-26.2-15.jar) |

Every cell ran **five times** with a 90-second cooldown between runs, and the order of servers was rotated between repeats so no build always ran on a warm or a cold machine. The numbers below are the **median of the five runs**; *run spread* is (max − min) / median of those five. Paper is the baseline, and a negative percentage means less time than Paper.

## Profiles

Paper and Purpur ran as shipped. DivineMC ran in several configurations of `divinemc.yml`:

| Profile | What it changes |
|---|---|
| `stock` | nothing: DivineMC exactly as downloaded |
| `rct` | [regionized chunk ticking](/docs/divinemc/features/regionized-chunk-ticking) on, parallel world ticking off, nothing else |
| `max` | parallel world ticking, regionized chunk ticking, async chunk sending, [parallel sensors](/docs/divinemc/features/parallel-sensor-phase), async pathfinding, [parallel entity tracker](/docs/divinemc/features/parallel-entity-tracker), DAB, the density function compiler and compact bit storage |
| `parity` | every async and parallel feature off, vanilla region files: the same threading model as Paper |

`parity` is not a configuration anyone should run. It is there to show where the gains come from: with the threading turned off, DivineMC is 8% slower than Paper in botswarm, so the gains in the other profiles come from the parallel work.

::callout{type="info"}
  `max` turns on DAB (dynamic activation of brain), which ticks the AI of distant mobs less often. That is a change in behaviour, not only in speed; keep it in mind when comparing `max` with the other profiles.
::

## Many players: botswarm

150 SoulFire bots walk a 160-block grid in a world of radius 1,600 while 1,500 mobs are summoned among them. 120 seconds of warmup, then 300 seconds of measurement.

::BenchChart{scenario="botswarm"}
::

| Server | p50, ms | p99, ms | p50 vs Paper | p99 vs Paper | CPU, cores | CPU vs Paper | Budget left |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DivineMC rct | 15.41 | 21.72 | −58.9% | −60.3% | 1.78 | +31.1% | 69% |
| DivineMC max | 17.17 | 24.72 | −54.2% | −54.9% | 1.60 | +15.9% | 66% |
| DivineMC stock | 33.07 | 43.12 | −11.8% | −21.3% | 1.47 | +7.1% | 34% |
| Paper | 37.49 | 54.77 | — | — | 1.37 | — | 25% |
| Purpur | 38.48 | 55.61 | +2.6% | +1.5% | 1.40 | +2.1% | 23% |
| DivineMC parity | 40.61 | 59.77 | +8.3% | +9.1% | 1.46 | +6.4% | 19% |

*Budget left* is the share of the 50 ms tick that stays free at p50: how much more load the server can take before it drops below 20 TPS.

Both RCT and `max` more than double the headroom Paper has. They also hold the tail: DivineMC's p99 stays under 25 ms, while Paper's and Purpur's go past the 50 ms budget, so under this load they already fall behind now and then. Stock DivineMC is 12% ahead of Paper on p50 and 21% on p99 before any configuration.

## Separated regions: rctclusters

64 bots and 4,000 mobs in eight clusters 768 blocks apart, the case regionized chunk ticking is built for: every cluster can tick on its own thread. 120 seconds of warmup, 300 seconds of measurement. DivineMC ran with 4, 8 and 12 RCT worker threads; Paper and Purpur have no such setting and ran once.

::BenchChart{scenario="rctclusters"}
::

| Server | p50, ms | p99, ms | p50 vs Paper | p99 vs Paper | CPU, cores | CPU vs Paper |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DivineMC rct, 4 threads | 8.40 | 12.30 | −61.2% | −59.0% | 0.97 | +5.9% |
| DivineMC rct, 8 threads | 8.96 | 13.25 | −58.6% | −55.8% | 1.04 | +16.8% |
| DivineMC rct, 12 threads | 9.32 | 13.75 | −57.0% | −54.2% | 1.08 | +21.4% |
| Paper | 21.66 | 29.99 | — | — | 0.90 | — |
| Purpur | 21.87 | 32.72 | +1.0% | +9.1% | 0.95 | +4.8% |

Four threads already give the full effect for only 6% more CPU than Paper. Going to 8 or 12 threads does not make the tick faster, the differences are within the run spread, but it does cost more CPU. Start with `executor-thread-count` at 4 and raise it only if your regions are busier than these.

## Chunk generation: chunkgen

[Chunky](https://github.com/pop4959/Chunky) pregenerates a radius of 2,048 blocks, 66,049 chunks, on a fresh world every run. There are no players; the server gets the whole CPU.

::BenchChart{scenario="chunkgen"}
::

| Server | Time, s | Chunks/s | Time vs Paper | CPU-seconds | CPU vs Paper |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DivineMC max | 87.2 | 757 | −7.0% | 782 | −15.3% |
| DivineMC stock | 93.8 | 704 | 0.0% | 914 | −1.0% |
| Paper | 93.8 | 704 | — | 923 | — |
| Purpur | 94.0 | 703 | +0.2% | 924 | +0.1% |
| DivineMC parity | 94.6 | 698 | +0.9% | 910 | −1.5% |

The gain here is not parallelism: the density function compiler in `max` makes terrain generation itself cheaper, so the server finishes sooner *and* spends less CPU. Without it, all servers generate at the same speed within 1%.

## Reproducing

The raw runs, one JSON per scenario with every run's numbers, are published in the [mc-bench repository](https://github.com/NONPLAYT/mc-bench/tree/master/results/9950x3d/2026-09-28) together with the exact `bench.local.conf` and `builds.txt` of this host. The harness README explains how to run the same matrix on your own hardware. Results will differ on other machines; the relative order is what these runs are meant to show.
